**Manuscript title:** Supervision-Matched Selection of Paired RAG Repairs: An Empirical Study of Accuracy and Damage

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, “Supervision-Matched Selection of Paired RAG Repairs: An Empirical Study of Accuracy and Damage.” The manuscript studies a practical empirical question in retrieval-augmented question answering: after an original and a repaired answer have both been generated, which label-free signals help decide whether to execute the repair without unnecessarily replacing an answer that was already correct?

Across 6,000 question groups and 18,000 traces from three multi-hop QA datasets and three retrieval conditions, we hold candidate pairs, development supervision, eligibility, and a global 900-action allocation fixed. The main result is deliberately bounded. A calibrated HGB-plus-GbV selector improves exact match and reduces full-population Damage relative to a supervision-matched GbV-only selector. The same joint rule does not pass against the HGB-only selector, and a larger feature stack does not establish an advance. We believe the matched controls, full harm accounting, and retained negative comparisons provide a useful empirical boundary for selective RAG repair.

The manuscript does not claim a new selector architecture, state-of-the-art performance, reader-general transfer, or a deployment-cost saving. An anonymous aggregate verification package has been built and validated; its release status will follow the selected project license and the journal’s policy.
